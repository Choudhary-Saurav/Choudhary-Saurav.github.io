Project 01 · Genomics & stem cell biology

Building the genome, studying the cells, and mapping the spaces of Hymenolepis diminuta

In collaboration with Dr. Tania Rozario and Dr. Casey Bergman

PacBio HiFi Genome assembly Annotation scRNA-seq snRNA-seq Spatial transcriptomics

This is a collaborative, multi-aim project focused on building foundational genomic and single-cell resources for H. diminuta and using them to better understand regeneration and stem cell biology in tapeworms.

Aim 1 · Genome

Chromosome-level reference genome

I built an end-to-end assembly and annotation pipeline that produced a chromosome-scale reference genome for H. diminuta from PacBio HiFi reads, with repeat annotation (RepeatModeler/RepeatMasker), gene prediction (BRAKER3), and synteny analysis, providing a high-quality reference for future studies and insights into chromosome evolution in platyhelminths.

bioRxiv preprint NCBI assembly
Aim 2 · Single-cell

Comprehensive single-cell atlas

Mapping the cellular composition of H. diminuta and the heterogeneity of stem cells in the germinative region. Using single-cell and single-nucleus RNA-seq, we aim to characterize stem cell states linked to the parasite’s regenerative ability and discover novel cell subtypes. This resource will support research in planarian biology and parasitology, and may help establish H. diminuta as a model for studying human tapeworm infections.

Aim 3 · Spatial

Stem cell niche in space

Using spatial transcriptomics to reveal where key genes are expressed and to define the stem cell niche in the germinative region. This will show how surrounding signals shape stem cell behavior, with the goal of identifying novel pluripotent stem cell populations, or uncovering how multiple stem cell types and their environments may exhibit pluripotent behavior under specific niche conditions.

Project 02 · Statistical & functional genomics

Linking genetic risk to cellular function: a single-cell approach to understanding PUFA metabolism

Ye Lab, University of Georgia

GWAS scRNA-seq scATAC-seq Spatial transcriptomics Fatty acid traits

The question

How does genetic variation influence fatty acid traits, and how do these signals connect to specific cell types and biological pathways? Using large-scale human genomics data, we integrate GWAS summary statistics with atlas-level single-cell datasets to move from “association signals” to more interpretable biology, identifying the cell types, genes, and regulatory programs most relevant to PUFA metabolism. The goal is to pinpoint where these traits are controlled in the body and highlight candidate mechanisms that could explain why individuals differ in fatty acid levels and related metabolic outcomes.

The approach

We work with single-cell RNA-seq, scATAC-seq, and spatial transcriptomics datasets, and apply computational frameworks that combine trait genetics with cell-type resolution. This includes building clean, reproducible pipelines for quality control, annotation, and downstream analysis, and producing results that are easy to interpret through clear visualizations and summaries. Ultimately, this project aims to provide a deeper, cell-type–specific view of PUFA biology and create a foundation for future work linking genetic variation to metabolic health and disease risk.

Project 03 · Regulatory genomics & deep learning

Inferring gene regulatory networks from single-cell multiome data

Ye Lab, University of Georgia

scRNA-seq scATAC-seq Multiome Deep learning GRNs

I am applying a deep learning–based framework to infer gene regulatory networks from single-cell multiome data (paired scRNA-seq and scATAC-seq) using atlas-scale human datasets, connecting chromatin accessibility to the transcription factors and target genes that define cell states.
